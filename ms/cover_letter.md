# Cover letter: Scientometrics

Dear Editors,

Please consider "Propagation of Error: Citations to Retracted Research" for publication in *Scientometrics* as a research article.

The paper asks two questions. How much does a retraction reduce citations to the retracted article relative to what it would otherwise have received, and how much do retracted articles continue to be cited? We answer the first with a census of 8,627 Retraction Watch retractions of research articles (published 2000–2019, retracted 2005–2022), each matched to up to five articles from the same journal and publication year on pre-retraction citations, with OpenAlex citation histories through 2025 for both arms, and a stacked Poisson event study with article and stack-by-year fixed effects. Citations fall by about three fifths in the three years after retraction and the gap keeps widening for a decade; the result survives an unmatched journal-year comparison, synthetic difference-in-differences by cohort, and Rambachan–Roth sensitivity bounds for the small pre-retraction slippage we observe and report. We answer the second descriptively: 27% of all citations to these articles arrived after the retraction year, 39% of articles are still being cited five years on, and in a hand-coded sample 91% of post-retraction citing passages record no concern with the cited article.

Relative to the closest prior work (Furman, Jensen & Murray 2012; Lu et al. 2013; Shuai et al. 2017; Kühberger, Streit & Scherndl 2022), the contribution is a census across fields rather than a biomedical sample, open data and code so every number can be rebuilt, a design that reports its own pre-trends and sensitivity, heterogeneity by reason, field, time to retraction and prior expression of concern, and the descriptive level of continued citation set beside the causal penalty.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All data, code and generated results are public at https://github.com/recite/propagation_of_error. We used a large language model to assist with code and drafting, as stated in the declarations.

Sincerely,
Ken Cor and Gaurav Sood
